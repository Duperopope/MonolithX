## Why I started

I have a PC. I have a TV. And I wanted to play in my living room without buying another console just for that.

I discovered Moonlight, started putting an interface together, and kept adding the things I missed day to day. Finding my games in one place. Checking their updates without going from store to store. Following creators, watching a stream or a game’s story as a movie, rediscovering what was already in my library instead of buying more.

That became Monolith X. I use it at home and work on it in my spare time. Now I want to make it easy enough to install so other people can try it, tell me where it gets stuck, and help shape what comes next.

## Reviews deserve more than a number

![Player reviews and a game summary](docs/images/07-avis.png)

I want to understand what players enjoy and what annoys them. Monolith X brings feedback together and offers an AI-assisted summary. That doesn’t guarantee unbiased reviews: the sources and their limits matter as much as the summary.

## What makes it work

**The PC runs the games → Sunshine sends the picture → Moonlight receives it.** Monolith X provides the interface and content around that chain.

The project includes a Tizen client and an Android companion. You need a suitable local network and a compatible client; not every hardware configuration is supported yet.

The screenshots on this page come from my personal setup. They show the product with a library and accounts already configured; a fresh installation starts with the content detected on your own machine.

## Try the test build

This is the Monolith X project showcase. Source code and test packages remain private for now.

The setup was tested in an openSUSE Tumbleweed VM: installation, Sunshine Flatpak detection, launch through Moonlight and mouse navigation. Testing on other people’s machines is still ahead.

Want to test it or get involved? Share your setup and what interests you in an [issue](https://github.com/Duperopope/MonolithX/issues/new/choose). There is no public download yet.

## Help move the project forward

Useful feedback means your setup, what you tried, what you expected and what actually happened. The built-in form lets you prepare that feedback and export it as JSON. Online submission needs a configured collection server; it isn’t enabled in this build.

I use AI a lot when developing. I don’t hide it. I studied computing and I work on problems I can reproduce, but there’s still work to do before my personal setup becomes something anyone can install without a headache.

I’m looking for testers, other enthusiasts to work with, and people interested in the project or my background. Practical advice is welcome, including on Codex, Claude, local models and tools that actually help you.
